# Provenance standard — Section 4 transport model

**Adopted 2026-08. Non-negotiable.**

> Every single number needs to be directly verifiable and citable for a discerning and
> adversarial reviewer.

This document defines the three states every number in this repository must end in, records
what "lit-representative" was and why it was abolished, and reports the coverage actually
achieved, category by category, together with the numbers that remain assumptions and the
conclusion each one threatens.

The machine-readable registry is `data/parameters_provenance.csv`, generated by
`data/build_param_tables.py`. It renders into the SI as Tables S7a–S7k (§S9). **Do not hand-edit
the CSV** — edit the generator, which enforces the schema at build time.

---

## 1. The three states

Every number ends in exactly one of these. There is no fourth.

### A — `measured`
A specific external source **with a locator**: author / journal / year / volume / page, or
handbook + edition + section, page or table. A reviewer must be able to open the source and see
the number.

- Sufficient: *"CRC Handbook of Chemistry and Physics, 97th ed., Sect. 6, 'Viscosity of Liquids',
  pp. 6-243 to 6-247, 25 °C column"*; *"Eisenberg, Tobias & Wilke, J. Electrochem. Soc. 1954,
  101, 306–320"*.
- **Not** sufficient: *"CRC Handbook"*; *"Izutsu, Electrochemistry in Nonaqueous Solutions"*;
  *"Atobe/Noel flow-electrochemistry literature"*; *"quaternary-ammonium/organic-solvent
  conductivity class data"*.

The generator asserts that no `measured` row can be emitted with an empty `locator`.

### B — `derived`
Computed by an explicitly named, citable method from inputs that are **themselves state A or B**.
The method carries a citation with locator, and the arithmetic must be reproducible from the
registry alone.

A bound is not a derivation. If a method yields a range that contains the tabled value but does
not produce it, the row is state C with that range as its sensitivity — not state B.

### C — `assumption`
No source exists, or none could be page-anchored. Must be declared as an assumption **and** carry
a sensitivity statement giving the range tested and the conclusion that depends on it.

**If a conclusion flips inside the range, the conclusion is softened or cut.** The range is never
narrowed to save the conclusion. Two conclusions were found to flip in this pass; both were
weakened (§4 below).

The generator asserts that no `assumption` row can be emitted with an empty `sensitivity`.

## 2. `lit-representative` is abolished

The retired vocabulary was: `measured-lit`, `lit-representative`, `correlation-est`, `derived`,
`assumption`, `numerical`.

`lit-representative` meant *"a plausible magnitude for this class of system, tolerant to a factor
of about two."* It is not distinguishable from an invented number by a reader who cannot check it,
and the tolerance attached to it was a blanket assertion that was never tested per row. When it
was tested here it turned out to be **false**: the boilerplate string *"i²L/κ conclusions robust
to 2×"* appeared on all 49 conductivity rows, and the DMF entry that carries the Fig. K
microfluidic statement has a margin of **1.08×**.

All 86 rows that carried the class have been re-derived into A, B or C. The other retired classes
were folded in: `correlation-est` → `derived` (a named correlation from A/B inputs is a
derivation); `numerical` → `assumption` (a solver setting is a declared choice, with the audit
gate that bounds it quoted as its sensitivity).

### Two new columns

| column | rule |
|---|---|
| `locator` | page / section / table / equation. Empty **only** for `derived` and `assumption`. |
| `sensitivity` | Mandatory on every `assumption`. Also used on A/B rows to record a residual exposure (e.g. an extrapolation outside a correlation's calibration range). |

### Rules of practice

1. **Never invent or guess a citation.** If no source can be found, say so and route the number
   to state C with an honest sensitivity. A wrong citation is far worse than a declared
   assumption.
2. **A citation that does not support its value is withdrawn, not re-paginated.** Three were
   withdrawn in this pass (§5).
3. **Name the pull.** Every state-C row that *could* be upgraded says explicitly what to open. The
   `method_note` of those rows begins `PULL NEEDED:`.
4. **State C is not a failure state.** A declared assumption with a tested band is stronger than a
   plausible-looking citation that does not survive being opened.

| 2. Solvents | 100 | 34 | 48 | 18 |

| 4. Solver species diffusivities | 27 | 1 | 11 | 15 |

| 6. Electrolyte conductivities | 49 | 0 | 0 | 49 |
| 7. Reactors | 11 | 4 | 1 | 6 |
| 8. Voltage stack | 3 | 0 | 1 | 2 |
| 9. Thermal model | 41 | 4 | 13 | 24 |
| 10. Homogeneous kinetics | 1 | 0 | 0 | 1 |
| 11. Numerics | 7 | 0 | 0 | 7 |
| **Total** | **268** | **64 (23.9%)** | **79 (29.5%)** | **125 (46.6%)** |

**A + B = 53.4%**, against 25.3% tracing to any external source and 15.1% to a specific page
before this pass.

The 46.6% in state C is the honest figure and should not be read as a defect count. Two
structural facts, each verified independently of the sourcing, bound what it costs.

**κ is not on the transport path.** `i_lim` (Eq. S1) and the Nernst–Planck / EC′ solvers use
*D*, *C*, δ and *z* only. κ appears solely in `julia/cellvoltage.jl` and the voltage/thermal
figure scripts (`make_figK.py`, `make_fig4B.py`, `make_figs.py` Fig. D, `make_fig_main.py` panel
f). **45 of the 49 conductivities are display-only** — they support no stated conclusion. The
whole of category 6, which is 39% of the state-C population, is therefore a labelling problem
rather than a physics exposure, except for four rows.

**h_int is not load-bearing.** Because U′ = [(1/h_int + 1/h_ext)⁻¹]·σ with h_ext ≈ 13 W m⁻² K⁻¹,
the external film is 87–99.7% of the series resistance. Sweeping h_int from 50 W m⁻² K⁻¹ to
infinity — a range above 2×10⁴, spanning the entire Incropera Table 1.1 liquid band — moves every
boil-off ceiling by −5/+6%. That retires 4 of the 24 state-C thermal rows at a stroke.

### Category notes

- **Category 6 is 0/49 at A or B, and that is the finding, not a gap in effort.** No measured κ
  for the exact salt, solvent and concentration of any entry in this work exists in the accessible
  literature. Nor can these be derived: a limiting-conductivity table cannot supply κ at
  0.03–1 M — for Bu₄NBF₄/MeCN the Onsager limiting law, evaluated with the verified
  Λ° = 171.1 S cm² mol⁻¹ and S = 358.7, goes negative above 0.228 M, and the Lee–Wheaton /
  Fuoss–Justice extension reaches only ~0.02 M. Exactly one registry row (0.01 M Bu₄NPF₆/HFIP) is
  dilute enough for the limiting route, and HFIP has no λ° table. The inherited Izutsu citation
  therefore could not have supported any of the 29 rows it was attached to, at any concentration.
- **Category 2 is 34 A / 48 B / 18 C.** The pure-solvent μ and ρ are page-anchored to the CRC 97th
  edition. Every mixture μ and ρ, and both HFIP entries, are assumptions. Only the product φ·M
  enters Eq. S2, so for mixtures the tabulated "M" is not a molar mass but half of a
  presentational split of a Perkins–Geankoplis product — stated in the registry and in the Table
  S3 caption so that 36.4 or 26.4 g mol⁻¹ cannot be misread as a molecular weight.
- **Category 4** aqueous rows are all state B: D = RTλ°/(z²F²) reproduces every tabulated value to
  the digits shown from the CRC/Vanýsek λ°. The one non-aqueous row that reaches B is Br⁻(MeCN),
  from the page-anchored λ° = 102.00 S cm² mol⁻¹ of Kalugin 2019 Table 3, p. 28, which reproduces
  2.716×10⁻⁹ m² s⁻¹ exactly.
- **Category 11** is 7/7 state C by construction: a solver setting is a declared choice. Each row
  quotes the audit gate that bounds it.

## 5. Citations withdrawn because they do not support their value

These are recorded rather than silently repaired, and each is stated in the registry
`method_note`.

1. **Bard & Faulkner, 2nd ed., ch. 1** — cited for δ = 300 µm (unstirred natural convection). The
   book contains **no numerical δ** for natural convection: §1.4.2 (pp. 29–31) defines δ₀ and
   states it is "often unknown"; §1.4.3 gives only the transient δ(t) = 2√(Dt); §4.4.2 (p. 149)
   gives only the √(2Dt) rule of thumb. Replaced by a derived free-convection band (Wilke,
   Eisenberg & Tobias, *J. Electrochem. Soc.* **1953**, *100*, 513–523) with a measured
   cross-check (Katona et al., *J. Electrochem. Soc.* **2021**, *168*, 031512). Row → C.
2. **Pletcher & Walsh, *Industrial Electrochemistry*, 2nd ed.** — cited for δ = 100 µm at
   ~500 rpm. No supporting passage could be located, and the citation carried no chapter, page or
   table. Row → C, and the reference has been **deleted from the SI reference list** (it was cited
   nowhere else).
3. **"Wallnoefer-Ogris et al., *Front. Chem. Eng.* 2024, 6, 1384772"** — cited for U′ = 0.30
   W cm⁻² K⁻¹ and for "2–6 W cm⁻² rejected across 10–20 K gradients." That volume and article
   number is a **different paper** (Eichner, Amiri, Burheim & Lamb), and it reports 0.32–0.42
   W cm⁻² at 2 A cm⁻² with a coolant h of 45 W m⁻² K⁻¹, implying U′ ≈ 0.026 — an order of
   magnitude below the claim. The citation was a chimera of two sources. Replaced by a derivation
   from laminar internal flow (Incropera 6th ed., Table 8.1 / Eq. 8.53), which brackets 0.30 at
   0.09–0.49 W cm⁻² K⁻¹. The SI's "1–10 W cm⁻² routinely rejected" was corrected to 0.3–2.5.

Also corrected, without a withdrawal:

- **CRC "viscosity/density tables, 25 °C"** was pasted down a whole column and attached to heat
  capacities, boiling points and conductivities, which are in different sections entirely. Each
  now carries its own section locator; heat capacities moved to the NIST WebBook.
- **CRC "Concentrative Properties of Aqueous Solutions"** was cited for mixture μ and ρ at 25 °C
  and a v/v ratio. That table is at **20 °C** and indexed by mass per cent. Withdrawn; those rows
  → C.
- **The CRC conductivity table stops at 0.1 mol L⁻¹** and cannot support any of the five
  concentrated aqueous κ rows (1–2 M) that cited it. All → C.
- **"Atobe/Noel flow-electrochemistry literature"** named no paper. Replaced by Noël, Cao &
  Laudadio, *Acc. Chem. Res.* **2019**, *52*, 2858–2869 and Watts, Gattrell & Wirth, *Beilstein J.
  Org. Chem.* **2011**, *7*, 1108–1114 (254 µm FEP spacer) — which promotes the 250 µm gap to
  state A.
- **`UA still air` = 0.20 W K⁻¹** was not reproducible from its own stated inputs (it implies
  h ≈ 16 W m⁻² K⁻¹). Corrected to the derived **0.1625 W K⁻¹** = 13.0 × 0.0125.
- **`Vessel external area` = 0.0125 m²** was classed `derived` from a cylinder that holds 157 mL,
  not 100 mL. Reclassified C with the wetted-area alternative (0.00996 m²) as its sensitivity.
- **`U′ stirred bath` = 0.18 W cm⁻² K⁻¹** contradicts Fig. K's own stirred-beaker U′ = 0.0160 by
  11×. Re-scoped as a *jacketed-glass* reference case — a different architecture from any of Fig.
  K's five — and explicitly not the Fig. K stirred beaker.
- **`i₀` "insensitive above ~10 mA cm⁻²"** is true only where ohmic heat dominates. At the
  zero-gap stack it moves ceilings −44/+31% over 0.01–10 mA cm⁻². Registered with the real sweep.

## 6. Open pulls, in order of yield

1. **Dorn, Kareth, Weidner & Petermann, *J. Chem. Eng. Data* 2024, 69, 1493–1502** — Casteel–Amis
   fits of measured κ(c) isotherms for 164 electrolytes in water/MeCN/MeOH/EtOH. Its SI may
   convert 8–10 conductivity rows to state A in a single pass. (ACS returned 403 throughout this
   pass; nothing is quoted from it.)
2. **Measure 0.2 M NaI in dry DMF.** Conductivity probe, ten minutes. It is the only number in the
   registry whose conclusion has an 8% margin.
3. **Krumgalz, *J. Chem. Soc. Faraday Trans. 1* 1983, 79, 571–587** — limiting ionic conductances
   in 50 organic solvents. Would move several category-4 rows from C to B.
4. **An agitated-vessel Sherwood correlation** with author/journal/volume/page and a stated
   validity range, for δ (stirred batch). This is the single highest-value literature pull in the
   repository: it is the only rung of the archetype ladder that is asserted rather than computed,
   and it is the input that already forced one conclusion to be weakened.
5. **Chambers, Stokes & Stokes, *J. Phys. Chem.* 1956, 60, 985–986** (2 M NaCl) and **Darling,
   *J. Chem. Eng. Data* 1964, 9, 421–426** (2 M H₂SO₄) — display-only, but cheap.
6. **Aminabhavi & Gopalakrishna 1995 / González et al. 2007 / Cunningham, Vidulich & Kay 1967** —
   25 °C mixture isotherms, to move 16 mixture μ/ρ rows from C to B.

   pp. 875–882** for the Lévêque coefficient 1.85; **Eisenberg, Tobias & Wilke 1954** for the RCE
   Re and Sc validity ranges; a **named separator** (Nafion 115/117/212) with a page-anchored

## 8. Maintaining the standard

- Edit `data/build_param_tables.py`, never the CSV. Its `add()` asserts the state vocabulary,
  a locator on every `measured` row and a sensitivity on every `assumption` row, and it fails the
  build otherwise.
- `data/electrolytes.csv` carries `state` and `status` columns so no κ can be consumed without its
  class travelling with it. `status = unused-legacy` marks the 18 entries that are defined in
  `build_reactions50.py` but assigned to no reaction and therefore carry no registry row at all;
  do not adopt one into a figure without registering it first.
- Rebuild: `cd data && python build_param_tables.py && node ../make_si.js`. The SI build runs its
  own citation audit (reference order and no uncited references) and must exit 0.
- When adding a constant to a figure script, add its registry row in the same commit. The Fig. K
  scripts (`figs/thermal_model.py`, `figs/make_figK.py`) carry a provenance block in their
  docstrings listing every constant and its state; keep it in sync.
